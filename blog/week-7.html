Week 7: The Knowledge Federation: Ingesting Agribalyse, Statutory Allergens, and Clinical Hypersensitivities

In Week 6, we solved a fundamental vulnerability in our voice assistant: we eradicated hallucinated macronutrients by grounding our local Qwen 2.5 1.5B orchestrator in France's official ANSES CIQUAL nutrition database. By establishing the ironclad rule that the language model must never generate raw SQL queries, our Orange Pi 5 could accurately state calories, proteins, carbohydrates, and fats in sub-millisecond lookups.

Yet as our small hardware bench began using VITA for daily meal logging, our queries immediately stretched beyond standard macros:

  • "What is the environmental carbon footprint of 200g of ribeye steak versus a lentil patty?"
  • "I have an anaphylactic peanut allergy. Is this satay or pesto dressing safe for me?"
  • "I have severe birch pollen hay fever. Why do raw apples make my lips and palate swell, but applesauce causes zero reaction?"
  • "I am diagnosed with lactose intolerance. Can I safely eat aged cheddar cheese?"

A single nutrition database cannot answer these questions. Worse, relying on a generative LLM's parametric weights for clinical allergies is hazardous—a hallucinated allergen prediction can trigger anaphylactic shock. To solve this, Week 7 became our Knowledge Federation Sprint: building an immutable, multi-database factual foundation where ecological life-cycle metrics, statutory allergen crosswalks, and clinical pollen-food cross-reactivities are compiled into synchronized SQLite artifacts on edge hardware.

The 4 Authoritative Databases Explained

Rather than dumping unrelated datasets into an untracked repository, we designed a modular compilation architecture under database_builders/. Every database represents a distinct factual domain, compiled from authoritative government or clinical sources, and anchored to a common primary identifier.

Figure 1: The CIQUAL-First Knowledge Build Pipeline. Downstream databases link to CIQUAL's authoritative alim_code; orphaned references are rejected by Gate 1A.

1. ANSES CIQUAL 2025: The Master Anchor

As detailed in Week 6, CIQUAL provides 3,483 authoritative food items with 74 nutritional constituents and 257,742 chemical composition measurements. But CIQUAL's most important architectural role in Week 7 is serving as the ecosystem anchor. Every food entry carries an immutable integer identifier: alim_code.

By making alim_code our universal foreign key, we prevented the catastrophic entity fragmentation that plagues most nutrition apps (e.g., matching "Salmon fillet" in one DB to "Atlantic Salmon, grilled" in another via messy runtime string heuristics).

2. ADEME / INRAE Agribalyse 3.2: Environmental LCA & PEF 3.1 Eco-Scores

Consumers increasingly want to know the ecological cost of their diet. To provide mathematically verifiable environmental metrics, we integrated Agribalyse 3.2, published in August 2025 by France's ADEME and INRAE.

Agribalyse compiles rigorous Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) footprints adhering to the European Commission's Product Environmental Footprint (PEF) EF 3.1 standard. For every food item, it models 16 standardized midpoint indicators across six distinct lifecycle stages: Agriculture, Food Processing, Packaging, Transportation, Distribution, and Home Preparation.

Climate Change (Carbon) kg CO2 eq / kg 29.10 kg 1.68 kg 17.3x lower emissions

ETL & Data Transformation: The raw Agribalyse release comes as an unindexed 45MB Excel workbook containing dozens of merged headers and complex multi-scenario variations (raw vs. cooked, farm gate vs. consumer ready). Our agribalyse_builder/importer.py parses this artifact, normalizes lifecycle phases, maps the indicators into a lean 2.69MB SQLite database, and resolves scenarios using a deterministic ScenarioSelectionPolicy (prioritizing the lowest Data Quality Rating, or DQR score, for empirical certainty).

Enforcing Zero Orphan Invariants

During our initial import, 155 food products in the Agribalyse spreadsheet referenced French culinary concepts that lacked an exact match in CIQUAL 2025. Rather than tolerating dangling pointers, our builder strictly dropped all 155 orphan rows, guaranteeing that every single entry in database/agribalyse.db joins cleanly to a verified CIQUAL alim_code.

3. Statutory Food Allergens v2: EU 1169/2011 & US FDA FASTER Act

When an allergic user speaks to VITA, there is zero margin for error. We built database/food_allergens_v2.db to model statutory allergen disclosures with clinical rigor.

The builder compiles legal standards across two major jurisdictions:

  • European Union (Regulation 1169/2011 Annex II): 14 mandatory declarable allergen groups (Gluten-containing cereals, Crustaceans, Eggs, Fish, Peanuts, Soybeans, Milk/Lactose, Tree Nuts, Celery, Mustard, Sesame, Sulfur dioxide/Sulfites, Lupin, Molluscs).
  • United States (FDA FALCPA & FASTER Act 2021): 9 major food allergens (adding Sesame as the 9th major statutory group as of January 2023).

Upgrading to Schema v2 (Typed Evidence States): In our initial v1 prototype, allergens were simple binary flags (is_peanut = 1). In real clinical life, allergen contamination is far more nuanced. In Schema v2, we introduced typed evidence states across 2,695 food relationships:

  • positive (2,686 rows): Definitively containing the allergenic substance (e.g. Peanut butter containing peanuts).
  • uncertain (9 rows): Ingredient variations require explicit advisory warnings.
  • precautionary_labeling & cross_contact_risk: Factory line sharing and shared equipment risk.

Every profile query automatically injects a statutory disclaimer citation ("REGULATORY CITATION: EU Reg 1169/2011 Annex II / US 21 USC 321(qq)"), ensuring that VITA's spoken responses can be legally audited by public health compliance teams.

4. Clinical Hypersensitivities & Inhalants v2: Pollen-Food Syndrome & Metabolic Intolerances

The most scientifically complex database in our stack is database/hypersensitivities_and_inhalants_v2.db. It models two distinct medical phenomena documented by the European Academy of Allergy and Clinical Immunology (EAACI) and the WHO/IUIS Allergen Nomenclature Committee:

A. Pollen-Food Allergy Syndrome (PFAS / Oral Allergy Syndrome)

Millions of people with seasonal pollen allergies experience itching and swelling in their lips and throat when eating fresh fruit or nuts. This is caused by cross-reactive protein homologs: the immune system's IgE antibodies confuse plant food proteins with inhaled airborne pollens.

Figure 2: Biological Cross-Reactivity Engine. The system models epitope heat-lability, distinguishing between dangerous raw ingestion and safe cooked forms.

Our database models 8 inhalant allergen sources (Birch, Grass, Ragweed, Mugwort, Latex, Olive, Plane tree, and Alder) mapped across 43 cross-reactivity associations. Crucially, our schema includes a preparation_state field that tracks heat-lability: PR-10 proteins are heat-sensitive and denature during cooking, while Lipid Transfer Proteins (LTPs, such as peach Pru p 3) are heat-stable and resist boiling.

B. Non-IgE Clinical Metabolic Intolerances

Beyond allergic reactions, the database maps 2,093 foods against 7 clinical metabolic conditions: Celiac gluten sensitivity, Lactose intolerance, Histamine intolerance, Fructose malabsorption, FODMAPs, Salicylate sensitivity, and Sulfite sensitivity.

Importantly, our engine tracks quantitative measurements. For example, while raw whole milk (alim_code 19010) triggers severe lactose alerts, Aged Cheddar (alim_code 12726) is mapped with evidence_state="low_measurement" and quantitative_value=0.01 g/100g. Because bacterial aging consumes the lactose, the database recognizes that Cheddar is clinically safe for most lactose-intolerant adults.

The Read-Only KnowledgeFacade: Edge Performance

To expose these 4 distinct SQLite files to VITA without code duplication, we engineered the KnowledgeFacade service under core/knowledge/service.py. The facade guarantees three architectural invariants:

  1. Hardware-Level Immutability: All database handles are opened with file:...mode=ro URI flags and execute PRAGMA query_only = ON;. Write operations are physically blocked by the SQLite engine.
  2. Unified Food Profile: A single call to facade.get_food_profile(alim_code) queries CIQUAL for macros, Agribalyse for LCA carbon metrics, Food Allergens for regulatory evidence, and Hypersensitivities for OAS cross-reactivities in a unified data structure.
  3. Zero Garbage Collection Pressure: Connections persist across voice turns, reusing cached query plans.
# core/knowledge/repositories/base.py
class BaseReadOnlyRepository:
    def __init__(self, database_path: str | Path) -> None:
        self.database_path = Path(database_path).resolve()
        if not self.database_path.is_file():
            raise FileNotFoundError(self.database_path)

    def connect(self) -> sqlite3.Connection:
        # Enforce OS & SQLite C-library read-only isolation
        conn = sqlite3.connect(f"file:{self.database_path.as_posix()}?mode=ro", uri=True)
        conn.row_factory = sqlite3.Row
        conn.execute("PRAGMA query_only = ON;")
        return conn

By keeping the data on bare-metal SQLite rather than wrapping it in HTTP REST APIs, our embedded voice pipeline remains blazing fast.

Real-World Scenario Walkthroughs

Here is what happens inside VITA when real users query our federated knowledge base:

Scenario 1: Ecological Footprint Query

"What is the carbon footprint difference between 200g of ribeye steak and 200g of lentil patty?"

  • LLM Extraction: Extracts entity slots: [{"food": "ribeye steak", "portion": 200}, {"food": "lentil patty", "portion": 200}].
  • Facade Execution: Queries agribalyse.db for alim_code 6103 (29.1 kg CO2e/kg) and alim_code 25591 (1.68 kg CO2e/kg).
  • Deterministic Math: Computes 29.1 × 0.200 = 5.82 kg CO2e vs. 1.68 × 0.200 = 0.34 kg CO2e. Ratio: 17.3×.
  • Spoken Output: "A 200g ribeye steak emits 5.82 kg of CO2 equivalent, compared to just 0.34 kg for a 200g lentil patty—a 17-fold reduction in greenhouse gas emissions."

Scenario 2: Birch Pollen & Apple Cross-Reactivity

"I have a birch pollen allergy. Why do raw apples make my lips tingle, but applesauce is fine?"

  • LLM Extraction: Identifies user condition: inhalant:bet_v_1, foods: raw apple vs cooked apple.
  • Facade Execution: Probes food_pollen_cross_reactivity_map. For raw apple (13039), finds Pollen-food allergy syndrome (birch / PR-10) with raw_declared_in_food_name. For cooked apple (13176), finds 0 active records.
  • Spoken Output: "Birch pollen allergen Bet v 1 cross-reacts with the PR-10 protein Mal d 1 in raw apples, causing oral tingling. However, PR-10 proteins are heat-sensitive and denature during cooking, making cooked applesauce safe for you."

Looking Ahead: The Intent Explosion Dilemma

By the end of Week 7, we had achieved something extraordinary: our local edge assistant was grounded in 22MB of verified European government and clinical databases. It could calculate ecological footprints, verify statutory allergens, and explain complex pollen-food cross-reactions without a single arithmetic error or hallucination.

Yet as we connected all these capabilities to our conversational loop, a brand-new architectural crisis emerged. To enable the LLM to query all these specialized databases—nutrition, carbon footprints, statutory allergens, clinical intolerances, preparation states—we had to define a massive catalog of intents, tool definitions, and slot parameters.

Injecting this enormous intent dictionary into the system prompt at every user turn began consuming hundreds of tokens, bloating the context window of our local Qwen 2.5 1.5B model and slowing inference down. Worse, during safety audits, it was nearly impossible to explain deterministically why the LLM selected one complex intent over another. In Week 8, we would have to confront the limits of prompt-injected intent routers on edge hardware...
